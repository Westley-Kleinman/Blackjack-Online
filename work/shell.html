Step 1 of 4

What are you counting?

You are not adding up the points in your own hand. A running count is one small number that tracks cards everyone has seen from the shoe.

Start at 0 when a new shoe is shuffled. Change that number as cards appear. Keep it across hands; reset it only when the shoe is reshuffled.

Count your cards and the dealer’s visible card as they appear. Count the dealer’s face-down card only after it is turned over. The count is a practice tool, not a guarantee of winning.

Give each rank a tiny value. Suits do not matter. This Hi-Lo value is different from a card’s blackjack point value.

2, 3, 4, 5, 6+1Low card seen → add one
7, 8, 90Middle card seen → no change
10, J, Q, K, A−1High card seen → subtract one
Why does a low card add one? Once it is gone, the unseen cards contain a slightly higher share of 10s and aces, which can make blackjacks. A positive count points to relatively more high cards unseen; a negative count points to relatively more low cards unseen; zero is balanced. It does not predict the next card or guarantee a win.

Memory hook: low adds, middle stays, high subtracts.

How these side bets work

Each side wager requires a main bet. Only the highest matching category pays. A 9:1 win returns £9 profit plus the original £1 stake. Side bets settle from your first two cards and, where specified, the dealer’s upcard. Hits and splits do not create new side bets; a voided round refunds all wagers.

The displayed odds use the selected deck count at the start of a fresh shoe. Casinos can use different payout schedules and limits. The presets here follow Wizard of Odds Perfect Pairs version 1, 21+3 versions 1 and 7, and Lucky Lucky table 1; progressive jackpots are not included.
